Ethics & References

Our security education is designed to build trust, respect user choice, and improve access to safer digital experiences. The commitments below guide the portal’s content and sample activities.

Data Privacy Standards

Tracer has no accounts, no cookies, and no tracking. Learning progress is stored only in localStorage on this device. Password tool input stays in the page and is not saved or sent anywhere. Nothing leaves your device. Our hosting provider may keep standard server logs, such as IP addresses. This site itself sets no cookies and runs no analytics.

  • Clear purpose and consent
  • Data minimization
  • Local storage and user control
  • Transparent privacy practices

Accessibility Guidelines

We aim for WCAG 2.1 AA-aligned experiences with semantic structure, keyboard access, visible focus, readable contrast, and responsive layouts.

  • Keyboard navigation and skip link
  • Meaningful labels and image descriptions
  • Accessible status and progress information
  • High-contrast and text-size preferences

Ethics Framework

We evaluate content and activities using four commitments:

Integrity

Present accurate guidance, distinguish facts from examples, and correct errors promptly.

Transparency

Explain that learning data remains in local browser storage and that this site sends nothing off-device.

Accountability

Provide a path to report concerns, assign owners, and review outcomes responsibly.

Equity

Respect diverse needs, avoid blame, and make learning materials accessible and inclusive.

Inclusive design and reader choice

People read and use technology in different ways. Easy Reading is an optional presentation of the same learning pages, not a separate or reduced service. It offers larger text, more space between lines, clearly labelled controls, larger click targets, a short navigation list, and one lesson idea at a time.

Readers choose their mode themselves. Age ranges only offer a suggestion; the site never switches modes based on age. The welcome dialog can be dismissed with Escape, and the always-visible controls let people change mode and text size whenever they want.

Plain-language explanations, keyboard-accessible glossary definitions, lesson read-aloud where the browser supports speech, reduced motion, and a large-print safety checklist provide more than one way to understand and use the material. The scam checker runs with simple rules in the browser and does not send or save answers.

These choices aim to support different reading, vision, motor, and attention needs without making assumptions about a person or labelling them. They complement—not replace—keyboard testing, browser zoom, screen-reader testing, and feedback from people with varied access needs.
