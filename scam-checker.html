Common scam approaches

Family-emergency calls

A caller says a relative is in trouble and asks for money or secrecy. Call your relative or another family member using a number you already know.

Tech-support pop-ups

A warning says your device is infected and tells you to call or install a tool. Close the page and contact support using the device maker’s official app or site.

Fake bank or government calls

A caller threatens arrest or account loss and asks for money, codes, or details. Hang up and call the organization using a trusted number.

Romance scams

Someone you met online asks for money, gift cards, or help with an emergency. Do not send money; talk with someone you trust.

Delivery texts

A text says you must pay a fee or fix an address. Check the delivery in the carrier’s official app or website instead of its link.

Fake prizes

A message says you won but must pay or share details to claim it. Do not pay a fee; check the offer with the organization through a trusted route.
