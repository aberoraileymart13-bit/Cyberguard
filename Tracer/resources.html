Threat Wiki

A practical reference for recognizing harmful behavior and choosing a safe next step. If you feel at risk, preserve relevant evidence where safe and contact a trusted person or the appropriate support team.

Phishing

Messages that impersonate a trusted service to pressure you into sharing credentials, opening a file, or approving a request. Look for an unexpected ask, urgency, sender mismatch, and links that do not lead to the official domain.

Safer response: Open the service independently and report suspicious messages through a known channel.

Online Scams

Fraud may arrive as a fake investment, marketplace payment, romance request, technical-support call, or prize notification. Scammers often create urgency or ask for an upfront fee, gift cards, or cryptocurrency.

Safer response: Pause, verify through an independent source, and do not send money or codes under pressure.

Cyberbullying

Repeated online harassment can include threats, humiliation, impersonation, or sharing private content without permission. It is not your fault, and support is available.

Safer response: Use platform blocking and reporting tools, save evidence when safe, and reach a trusted adult, manager, or support professional.

Identity Theft

Someone may misuse personal or financial information to access accounts, make purchases, or impersonate you. Unexpected account alerts, unfamiliar transactions, or lost access can be warning signs.

Safer response: Contact the affected provider using official details, secure accounts from a trusted device, and follow your local reporting guidance.

Deceptive vs. legitimate: email

Potentially deceptive

“Immediate action required!” from a misspelled sender domain, with an unfamiliar sign-in link and a request for a one-time code.

More likely legitimate

A routine message from a verified address that does not ask for your password and directs you to open the official app to review details.

Deceptive vs. legitimate: websites

Potentially deceptive

A look-alike web address with extra characters, unexpected redirects, pressure to act, or requests for excessive personal details.

More likely legitimate

A correctly typed, independently verified domain with a secure connection and a request limited to the information needed for the service.

A padlock or polished design alone does not prove a site is trustworthy. Verify the domain and the request.
